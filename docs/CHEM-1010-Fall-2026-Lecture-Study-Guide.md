# How to Use This Guide

A key point from the instructor is:

> **"Due" dates are really "do" dates.**

In other words, the dates shown in the Canvas calendar generally
indicate **when the instructor intends for an activity to be worked
on**, rather than simply the last possible moment when it should be
submitted.

That makes the Canvas calendar useful as a **learning sequence**.

The course distributes learning across several environments rather than
following a single lecture-then-homework sequence. A useful working
model is:

**Prepare → Encounter/Explore → Apply → Consolidate → Deliberately
Practice → Assess**

**Recall, Reflect, and Verify** runs across that sequence over time as a
recurring retrieval, reflection, and verification layer. Critical
Chemistry, Khan Academy, Canvas resources, review PDFs, simulations, and
face-to-face instruction can each contribute to different stages.

A Pre-Reading activity generally establishes **exposure, not mastery**.
Likewise, face-to-face lecture is not necessarily the first or only
place where understanding develops; its highest-value role is often to
connect, clarify, model reasoning, diagnose misconceptions, and
integrate the learning students are doing across environments.

Therefore, the best approach is not to wait until 11:59 PM on the listed
date. Instead, use each calendar date to determine what material should
be studied **before or around the corresponding Tuesday/Thursday
lecture**.

## Section 1.0 --- Growing Your Math Skills

Section 1.0 is best understood as the course's
**quantitative-foundations section**, not as disconnected math review.
Students are learning how chemists represent, transform, and reason with
measured quantities.

### TUE SEP. 1 --- Measurement, Uncertainty & Significant Figures

#### Before Lecture

Students should have encountered:

-   Measurements and Metric System --- Pre-Reading and Quiz
-   Khan Academy metric-conversion practice
-   Khan Academy scientific-notation practice
-   Section 1.0 Recall, Reflect, and Verify
-   Significant Figures --- Pre-Reading and Quiz
-   Critical Chemistry Guided Reading --- Significant Figures

#### Teaching Focus

Connect the quantitative ideas already encountered:

-   a measurement as **number + unit + uncertainty**
-   SI units, metric prefixes, base and derived units
-   scientific notation and calculator fluency
-   exact versus measured quantities
-   accuracy and precision
-   significant figures as a representation of justified measurement
    precision
-   significant figures and rounding in calculations

The goal is not merely to teach digit-counting rules. Students should
connect significant figures to measurement uncertainty and understand
why exact quantities behave differently from measured quantities.

#### After Lecture

Continue the Section 1.0 sequence:

-   Algebraic Equations --- Pre-Reading and Quiz
-   Critical Chemistry Case Study --- Algebraic Equations
-   Dimensional Analysis --- Pre-Reading and Quiz
-   Critical Chemistry Dimensional Analysis activity, as assigned

### THU SEP. 3 --- Algebra, Proportional Reasoning & Dimensional Analysis

#### Before Lecture

Students should have encountered:

-   equation rearrangement and the balance method
-   PEMDAS / order of operations
-   conversion factors
-   dimensional analysis
-   units treated algebraically
-   density and other quantitative relationships

#### Teaching Focus

Establish a repeatable quantitative problem-solving routine:

-   identify what is given and what is wanted
-   determine the desired final units
-   rearrange equations to isolate the desired variable
-   distinguish direct and inverse relationships
-   map multi-step conversion pathways
-   orient conversion factors so units cancel
-   use density as a relationship and conversion factor
-   carry units throughout calculations
-   apply significant figures at the end
-   check whether units and magnitude are reasonable

The actual Section Quiz shows that students will need to combine these
skills in metric conversions, density problems, drug-dosage
calculations, scientific notation, and algebraic/proportional reasoning.

#### After Lecture

Complete the Section 1.0 learning cycle:

-   Section Quiz 1.0 --- Growing Your Math Skills
-   Consolidation Practice
-   Deliberate Practice

Then complete the Section 1.1 Recall, Reflect, and Verify activity over
the weekend.

# Best Study Strategy for This Course

Because the instructor uses Canvas dates as **"do dates,"** use the
calendar as a pacing guide rather than as a list of last-minute
deadlines.

## 1. Preview

Complete the **Pre-Reading and Quiz** before the corresponding lecture
whenever possible.

The goal is not necessarily to master the material beforehand. The goal
is to make the terminology and basic ideas familiar before the
instructor teaches them.

## 2. Attend Lecture

Use face-to-face class to **connect, clarify, practice, diagnose, and
integrate** what you are learning across Canvas, Critical Chemistry,
review resources, simulations, and other activities.

Pay particular attention to:

-   Why a procedure or model works
-   How the instructor connects ideas across resources and sections
-   How expert problem-solving decisions are made
-   Units and chemical notation
-   Common mistakes and misconceptions
-   Opportunities to attempt problems before seeing solutions

## 3. Practice Without Looking at Solutions

Use assigned learning activities---including:

-   Critical Chemistry Guided Readings
-   Case Studies
-   Problem Sets
-   simulations
-   supplemental practice

to determine whether you can actually apply the material yourself. These
activities may provide substantive instruction as well as practice; they
are not merely post-lecture homework.

## 4. Consolidate

Use **Consolidation Practice** to bring the section's ideas together.

## 5. Deliberately Practice Weak Areas

Use **Deliberate Practice** to work specifically on skills that still
cause difficulty.

Don't simply repeat problems you already know how to solve.

## 6. Take the Section Quiz

Treat the section quiz as a check:

**"Can I independently apply this section's concepts?"**

If the answer is no, identify exactly which skill is causing trouble
before moving on.

## 7. Retrieve, Reflect, and Verify Across Time

Use the recurring **Recall, Reflect, and Verify** activities as spaced
retrieval practice across current and earlier sections. Attempt from
memory before consulting resources, reflect on what the attempt reveals,
and verify afterward.

Do not treat retrieval as something that happens only after a section is
finished; the course deliberately brings earlier learning back into
later weeks.

## 8. Prepare for the Unit Test

Use the Unit Practice Test to locate weak areas across the entire unit.

Then revisit those particular concepts before taking the actual Unit
Test.

# One Rule to Remember

**Don't use 11:59 PM as your signal to begin.**

Use the Canvas date as the instructor saying:

**"This is what you should be working on now."**

A useful working cycle for this course is:

**Prepare → Encounter/Explore → Apply → Consolidate → Deliberately
Practice → Assess**

Across that cycle, continue to **retrieve → reflect → verify** earlier
learning through spaced practice.

Face-to-face class is one part of this distributed learning system, not
the sole source of instruction. Following the Canvas "do date" rhythm
should keep your work synchronized with the Tuesday/Thursday meetings
throughout CHEM 1010.
