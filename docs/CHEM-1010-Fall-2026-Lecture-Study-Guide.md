# How to Use This Guide

A key point from the instructor is:

> **“Due” dates are really “do” dates.**

In other words, the dates shown in the Canvas calendar generally indicate **when the instructor intends for an activity to be worked on**, rather than simply the last possible moment when it should be submitted.

That makes the Canvas calendar useful as a **learning sequence**.

The course generally follows this pattern:

**Pre-reading & quiz → Lecture → Guided reading/case study/problem set → Lecture/application → Consolidation & deliberate practice → Section quiz → Reflection → Unit assessment**

Therefore, the best approach is not to wait until 11:59 PM on the listed date. Instead, use each calendar date to determine what material should be studied **before or around the corresponding Tuesday/Thursday lecture**.

# Best Study Strategy for This Course

Because the instructor uses Canvas dates as **“do dates,”** use the calendar as a pacing guide rather than as a list of last-minute deadlines.

## 1. Preview

Complete the **Pre-Reading and Quiz** before the corresponding lecture whenever possible.

The goal is not necessarily to master the material beforehand. The goal is to make the terminology and basic ideas familiar before the instructor teaches them.

## 2. Attend Lecture

Use lecture to turn familiarity into understanding.

Pay particular attention to:

- Why a procedure works
- How the instructor sets up problems
- Units
- Chemical notation
- Common mistakes
- Examples the instructor emphasizes

## 3. Practice Without Looking at Solutions

Use the:

- Guided Readings
- Case Studies
- Problem Sets

to determine whether you can actually apply the material yourself.

## 4. Consolidate

Use **Consolidation Practice** to bring the section's ideas together.

## 5. Deliberately Practice Weak Areas

Use **Deliberate Practice** to work specifically on skills that still cause difficulty.

Don't simply repeat problems you already know how to solve.

## 6. Take the Section Quiz

Treat the section quiz as a check:

**“Can I independently apply this section's concepts?”**

If the answer is no, identify exactly which skill is causing trouble before moving on.

## 7. Reflect

Use the recurring **Recall, Reflect, and Verify** activities as an opportunity to retrieve concepts from memory rather than immediately looking them up.

## 8. Prepare for the Unit Test

Use the Unit Practice Test to locate weak areas across the entire unit.

Then revisit those particular concepts before taking the actual Unit Test.
