## 1. Why This Section Exists

Section 1.0 is the quantitative-foundations section for CHEM 1010. It
builds the measurement and mathematical tools students will repeatedly
use in later chemistry: units, scientific notation, significant figures,
algebraic rearrangement, proportional reasoning, and dimensional
analysis.

A useful conceptual center is:

``` text
measurement
    |
    +--> number + unit + uncertainty
    |
    +--> scientific notation / calculator fluency
    |
    +--> significant figures
    |
    +--> equations and proportional relationships
    |
    +--> dimensional analysis / unit cancellation
    |
    v
quantitative chemistry problem solving
```

> **Numbers in chemistry carry meaning through units, uncertainty,
> relationships, and conversion pathways.**

This is an instructor synthesis of the supplied course materials.

## 2. Why Section 1.0 Is Structurally Different

Sections 1.1 and 1.2 use Critical Chemistry as a major instructional
engine. Section 1.0 uses a **mixed resource architecture**:

-   Canvas Pre-Reading quizzes;
-   OpenStax-derived review PDFs;
-   Khan Academy videos and practice;
-   Critical Chemistry Guided Reading / Case Study activities;
-   Recall, Reflect, and Verify;
-   Consolidation Practice;
-   Deliberate Practice;
-   Section Quiz 1.0.

Khan Academy is explicitly supplemental and ungraded. Critical Chemistry
is used selectively for Significant Figures and Algebraic Equations.
Section 1.0 therefore appears designed as a **skills bootstrap** rather
than a conventional chemistry-content section.

## 4. Content Strands

### A. Measurement and the Metric System

Students encounter measurement as number, unit, and uncertainty; SI
units and prefixes; length, mass, volume, density, temperature, and
time; base and derived units; and basic conversions.

### B. Scientific Notation

Students express large/small quantities using powers of ten, convert
between standard and scientific notation, and use calculator conventions
such as `EE`, `EXP`, `e`, or `^`.

### C. Significant Figures

Students distinguish exact from measured quantities, connect instrument
uncertainty to reported digits, apply significant-figure rules, round
appropriately, and report calculated results at justified precision.

### D. Algebraic Equations

Students use a balance method to isolate variables, apply
reciprocal/opposite operations, maintain equality, use PEMDAS, and
rearrange relationships such as distance/rate/time, density, the ideal
gas law, and other chemistry formulas.

### E. Dimensional Analysis

Students treat units algebraically, construct conversion factors from
equivalences, orient factors so units cancel, perform multi-step
conversions, use density as a conversion factor, and solve applied
problems including travel and drug-dosage calculations.

### F. Proportional Reasoning

The Unit 1 outcomes and Section Quiz require reasoning about direct and
inverse relationships, including speed/distance/time, density, and
pressure/volume examples.

## 6. Role of the Components

### Measurements Pre-Reading

Introduces measurement, SI units/prefixes, derived units, density,
temperature, and basic conversions. Its 10-question quiz is primarily
recognition-level preparation.

### Khan Academy --- Metric Conversions

Supplemental and ungraded. Videos plus seven practice problems reinforce
powers-of-ten prefixes and metric conversion. Students may optionally
use this work in the Feynman Notes discussion.

### Khan Academy --- Scientific Notation

Supplemental and ungraded. Videos, seven practice problems, and a
calculator-use video develop scientific-notation and calculator fluency.

### Recall, Reflect, and Verify

Begins the course's recurring retrieval/metacognitive practice. The
Section 1.0 set integrates uncertainty, significant figures, dimensional
analysis, proportionality, exact numbers, algebra, and density.

### Significant Figures Pre-Reading

Introduces exact/measured numbers, uncertainty, accuracy/precision,
significant digits, calculation rules, and rounding. The actual
Pre-Reading quiz is mostly conceptual recognition.

### Critical Chemistry Guided Reading --- Significant Figures

Moves from recognition toward application: determining uncertainty,
applying significant-figure/rounding rules, performing calculations, and
explaining exact numbers.

### Algebraic Equations Pre-Reading

Introduces the balance method and variable isolation. Its actual quiz
applies these ideas to distance/rate/time, density, and the ideal gas
law.

### Critical Chemistry Case Study --- Algebraic Equations

Reinforces equations, PEMDAS, equivalence, balancing, and variable
isolation.

### Dimensional Analysis Pre-Reading

Introduces units as algebraic quantities, conversion factors,
cancellation, multi-step conversions, density conversions, travel cost,
drug dosage, and temperature conversion. The actual Pre-Reading quiz
primarily checks the conceptual framework.

### Consolidation Practice

Uses the standard retrieval-based Unit Test study-guide process. Its
listed Section 1.0 outcomes cover measurement, significant figures, and
algebra, but omit the explicit Dimensional Analysis outcomes.

### Deliberate Practice

Students select one challenging outcome and work a 10-problem solution
pathway using APAVR (Abstract/Plan, Attempt, Verify, Reflect).
Verification uses the COAST framework. Its eight selectable outcomes
provide a broader description of Section 1.0 than the Consolidation/Quiz
outcome list.

### Section Quiz 1.0

29 questions, two attempts. The actual quiz assesses substantially more
than its displayed 13-outcome list, including scientific notation,
metric prefixes, significant figures, density, multi-step unit
conversions, drug dosage, algebraic rearrangement, and proportional
reasoning.

## 8. Conceptual Story

### A. Measurements are not naked numbers

A scientific measurement includes a numerical value, a unit, and
uncertainty.

### B. Units carry mathematical information

Units should be multiplied, divided, and cancelled along with numbers.

### C. Powers of ten make scale manageable

Metric prefixes and scientific notation are related tools for handling
quantities across many orders of magnitude.

### D. Measurement uncertainty limits justified precision

Significant figures communicate what a measurement can support. Exact
counts and defined relationships behave differently from measured
quantities.

### E. Equations express relationships

Students must be able to isolate the desired variable rather than depend
on memorizing a separate formula for every situation.

### F. Dimensional analysis turns equivalence into a pathway

A conversion factor is a ratio equal to one. Properly oriented factors
remove unwanted units and leave desired units.

### G. Chemistry problems are often multi-step pathways

Success depends on identifying the target, mapping intermediate steps,
selecting relationships/conversion factors, preserving units,
calculating, and checking significant figures.

## 9. Proposed Role of Face-to-Face Instruction

> **INFERRED / INSTRUCTOR PRACTICE**

Section 1.0 is an especially good place to establish a consistent
quantitative problem-solving culture for the semester.

High-value classroom work includes:

-   requiring units at every step;
-   drawing a conversion map before calculation;
-   distinguishing exact versus measured quantities;
-   connecting sig figs to measurement uncertainty rather than teaching
    them only as digit-counting rules;
-   modeling calculator entry for scientific notation;
-   treating equations as relationships that can be rearranged;
-   making direct/inverse proportional reasoning explicit;
-   using dimensional analysis as the default multi-step conversion
    framework;
-   requiring students to inspect whether the final unit and magnitude
    make sense;
-   distinguishing arithmetic mistakes from setup/conceptual mistakes.

## 10. Proposed Tuesday/Thursday Rhythm

The Fall 2026 calendar spreads Section 1.0 across more than one
Tuesday/Thursday cycle, so it should not be forced into the two-meeting
pattern used for Sections 1.1 and 1.2.

### Early meeting --- Measurement, Metric System, Scientific Notation

Focus on number + unit + uncertainty; SI units/prefixes; base versus
derived units; metric scale; scientific notation; calculator fluency.

### Next meeting --- Significant Figures

Focus on exact versus measured numbers; uncertainty; accuracy/precision;
significant digits; rounding; and rules for calculations. Connect the
rules back to measurement rather than presenting them as arbitrary
procedures.

### Next meeting --- Algebra and Dimensional Analysis

Focus on variable isolation, proportional relationships,
conversion-factor orientation, unit cancellation, density as a
relationship/conversion factor, and multi-step pathways.

### End-of-section practice

Use mixed problems requiring students to combine skills: scientific
notation + sig figs + conversions + algebra + units.

## 11. A Recommended Quantitative Problem-Solving Routine

A consistent classroom routine could be:

``` text
1. What is given?
2. What is wanted?
3. What units must the answer have?
4. What relationship/equivalence connects them?
5. Map the pathway.
6. Set up factors/equation with units visible.
7. Solve.
8. Check unit cancellation.
9. Apply significant figures.
10. Ask whether the magnitude is reasonable.
```

This routine is an instructor-practice recommendation, not documented
inherited course wording.

## 14. How Section 1.0 Feeds Forward

Section 1.0 is infrastructure for nearly every later quantitative task.

Students will reuse:

-   units and dimensional analysis in conversions and stoichiometry;
-   significant figures in reported calculations;
-   algebra in gas laws, concentration, density, thermochemistry, and
    other equations;
-   scientific notation for atomic/molecular scales and concentrations;
-   proportional reasoning throughout quantitative chemistry;
-   density as both a physical property and a conversion relationship.

This explains why later Recall activities deliberately bring Section 1.0
problems back into Sections 1.1 and 1.2.

## 17. Instructor Quick Reference

**Purpose:** Build the quantitative language and problem-solving habits
students need for the rest of CHEM 1010.

``` text
measurement
   ↓
units + uncertainty
   ↓
scientific notation + sig figs
   ↓
algebraic relationships
   ↓
dimensional-analysis pathways
   ↓
quantitative chemistry
```

**Teaching emphasis:** do not present Section 1.0 as disconnected "math
review." Connect each skill to how chemists represent, transform, and
reason with measured quantities.

**Core habits:** show units, map pathways, isolate variables, cancel
units, preserve justified precision, and check reasonableness.
